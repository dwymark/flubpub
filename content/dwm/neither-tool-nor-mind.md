## Conversation as the unit of analysis

Given that the forward pass fails to meet Levin's notion of cognition, where does that leave us? At first glance, dead in the water. But first glances are often misleading. The instinct at this point is to disregard the idea in one of two ways: dismissal of cognition entirely as an LLM is "just doing statistics", or to hand-wave the issues with the particular analysis away and insist on LLM as mind regardless.

One move changes the game. That move is to locate the cognition in the the conversation itself. It's a non-conventional move that extends Levin's framework to a unit he hasn't considered (*footnote: It is possible Levin has considered something similar to this and I simply am unaware of the fact. To the best of my knowledge he hasn't; Levin is a productive researcher and I'm only familiar with a sliver of his total work*). Let's define the term *loop* to characterize the set of turn-taking information exchanges which, I will argue, defenda number of homeostatic set points. Refer to the collection of such set points as *coherence*. I claim that the traditional sense of the word "conversation" (a linguistic exchange between two or more human interlocutors) refers to a subset of the class of loops.

There is a field in Linguistics with a half-decade long history referred to as conversation analysis (CA). The researchers in this field provide us with a candidate for the homeostatic mechanism: conversation repair. There are many mechanisms of repair, for instance: catching oneself using the wrong term and correcting mid-turn ("I spoke with John, wait sorry, I mean Mary, last night"); one participant indicating a lack of comprehension ("Huh?", "What? I don't get that.", etc); third-position repair (Schlegoff 1992 (*PROVENANCE TODO: this citation is from Claude's traning data synthesis, verify it.*)) is when an interlocutor recognizes a misunderstanding in the other partner and corrects it ("A: Do you know the time? B: Three PM. A: No, I mean the time of the appointment."). All of these mechanisms serve to "keep the conversation on track", which is to say, to protect the coherence of the conversation. Loops involving a human and an LLM taking turns are no different: both interlocutors engage in conversation repair to defend coherence.
